# Client gift pack - specification and indicative cost plan (v1.0)

Purpose: a restrained, useful thank-you for property managers, managing agents, facilities managers and insurance handlers who place work with GBS. It is a working kit (notebook, pen, mug) rather than a novelty, presented in the brand colours with a hand-signed card. Nothing in it makes a claim; the welcome letter does the talking.

Reading the table: a core pack (everything except the optional food item) is roughly £22.85 to £38.90 per unit at 25, falling to about £14.73 to £26.00 at 100. Add the one-off set-up line once per production run.

Ways to bring the cost down if the upper range is quoted: use a printed card sleeve instead of a wrapped rigid lid; drop the belly band and rely on the sleeve; order mugs and pens in the 100 tier and hold stock (they do not date) while printing boxes and cards at 25 or 50.

## Assembly notes

1. Check every delivered item against the artwork and the sage sample. Reject mugs with print drift at the handle, notebooks with spine creep, and boxes with lifting wrap at the corners.
2. Lay out on a clean, dry surface. Line the base with one sheet of tissue, diagonal, corners up.
3. Place the notebook flat, cover up. Place the mug (in its own box or wrapped in the second tissue sheet) beside it, handle inward. Lay the pen along the notebook's spine edge.
4. Add the optional food item in its sealed pack on top of the notebook, and the contents card face up on top of everything.
5. Hand-write the recipient's first name and a short line inside the thank-you card, sign it, and place it on top, front up.
6. Fold the tissue over, fit the lid or sleeve, and apply the belly band with the strip logo centred on the lid. The band should be snug but not creased.
7. Record the recipient, organisation, date and who handed it over in the gift register (see below).

Keep a small stock of spare cards, tissue and bands; they are the items most often damaged.

## Handover notes

- Hand the pack over in person where possible, at a site visit, review meeting or the start of a new contract. Posting it is acceptable for remote clients; use a mailing carton one size up with void fill, never the gift box alone.
- The welcome letter goes in the pack only for new accounts; for existing clients replace it with a short covering note on a compliment slip.
- Many managing agents, local authorities, housing associations and insurers have gift and hospitality rules. Keep the value modest (the core pack is well under common £50 thresholds), include the optional food item only where you know it is acceptable, and never time a gift to coincide with a tender, a quotation decision or a claims settlement. If a recipient declines, accept without comment and record it.
- Keep a gift register: date, recipient, organisation, contents, value (use the mid-point of the cost table), handed over by, and any declaration the recipient's organisation asked for. The Bribery Act 2010 expects proportionate records; this register is it.
- Do not add branded clothing, vouchers or alcohol to the pack.
