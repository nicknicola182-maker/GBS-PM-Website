Planned and preventative maintenance

Scheduled programmes that keep buildings in good order, reduce emergency call-outs and give you a predictable maintenance budget.

Typical scope

  • Communal area inspections and minor repairs on a monthly or quarterly cycle
  • Cyclical internal and external redecoration programmes
  • Gutter, downpipe and roofline clearance and repair
  • Door, closer and ironmongery servicing across communal and plant room doors
  • Sealant, mastic, render and brickwork pointing repairs
  • Bin store, car park, pathway and boundary maintenance

What you receive

  • An annual programme with fixed visit rates and an agreed materials mark-up
  • A visit report with photographs after each attendance
  • A running defects log with priced recommendations for follow-on works
  • One point of contact for the whole portfolio

Reactive repairs

Fast, well-documented attendance for the day-to-day defects that occupied buildings generate, with a permanent repair rather than a repeat visit.

Typical scope

  • Make-safe attendance for leaks, damage and failed doors or locks
  • Plumbing leaks, overflow and waste repairs to communal services
  • Communal door, lock, closer and access control hardware repairs
  • Glazing and boarding up
  • Plaster, tiling, flooring and joinery repairs
  • Minor electrical and lighting works, with certified partners where statutory certification is required

Response categories

  • Emergency make-safe: attendance as quickly as possible, agreed per contract
  • Urgent: defects affecting security, weather-tightness or safe use
  • Routine: scheduled within the next programmed visit or an agreed window
  • Planned: grouped works priced as a single scope

Every attendance is followed by a job report: what we found, what we did, what remains outstanding and a price for any follow-on works.

Specialism

Fire door inspection, remedial works and installation

A fire door is an assembly, not a leaf. We assess each door against measurable tolerances and manufacturer instructions, recommend compliant repair before replacement wherever possible and provide the records a responsible person needs.

Inspection and reporting

  • Survey of fire door assemblies against BS 8214, Approved Document B and the manufacturer's installation instructions
  • Assessment of leaf and frame condition, lipping, gaps and threshold, intumescent strips and smoke seals, closers, hinges, locks and ironmongery, glazing and signage
  • Defect-by-defect report with photographs, measurements and a priority rating per door
  • Priced remedial schedule mapped to a clear cost plan, with options where there is a genuine choice

Remedial works and installation

  • Gap adjustment, lipping, seal replacement, closer and hinge replacement, glazing and signage
  • FD30 and FD60 door set supply and installation where a door cannot be brought back to standard
  • Flat entrance doors, communal corridor doors, riser and plant room doors

Maintenance and regulatory support

  • Maintenance programmes with inspection records supporting quarterly communal fire door checks and annual flat entrance door checks for buildings over 11 metres under the Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022
  • Door-by-door asset register for your fire safety file
  • Resident communication templates for flat entrance door access
  • Completion reports with before and after photographs for each door

Our approach: replacement is recommended only where a door cannot be repaired to a compliant standard. This keeps programmes proportionate and defensible for the leaseholders who ultimately fund them.

Fire door programme, step by step

  1. SurveyEvery door assessed and photographed on site.
  2. ReportDefects, measurements and priorities per door.
  3. Cost planPriced remedial schedule with options.
  4. Remedial worksRepairs and replacements to the agreed scope.
